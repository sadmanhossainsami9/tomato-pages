Tomato diseases

Know the leaf before it spreads

The 10 leaf classes the model learns to tell apart. Each one has a typical look, a cause, and ways to reduce it.

Fungal

Early blight

Alternaria solani
What it looks like

Dark brown spots with ring patterns, often with a yellow area around them. Usually starts on older, lower leaves.

When it spreads

Warm, humid weather with frequent leaf wetness.

How to reduce it

Rotate crops, remove infected leaves, mulch the soil, and water at the base instead of over the leaves.

Water mold

Late blight

Phytophthora infestans
What it looks like

Large, water-soaked patches that turn brown or dark and can dry into papery areas. White fuzzy growth may appear underneath in damp conditions.

When it spreads

Cool, wet weather. It can destroy a plant within days.

How to reduce it

Plant resistant varieties, leave space for airflow, and remove and dispose of infected plants quickly.

Fungal

Leaf mold

Passalora fulva
What it looks like

Pale green or yellow patches on the upper side of the leaf, with olive-green to brown velvety mold underneath.

When it spreads

High humidity and poor airflow, common in greenhouses and crowded plantings.

How to reduce it

Improve ventilation, space and prune plants, keep leaves dry, and choose resistant varieties.

Fungal

Septoria leaf spot

Septoria lycopersici
What it looks like

Many small, round spots with dark edges and tan or gray centers. Begins on lower leaves and moves upward.

When it spreads

Wet weather and splashing water that carries spores to the leaves.

How to reduce it

Remove affected lower leaves, avoid overhead watering, mulch, and rotate crops.

Bacterial

Bacterial spot

Xanthomonas bacteria
What it looks like

Small, dark, water-soaked spots that turn brown or black, sometimes with a yellow border. Leaves can look ragged as spots join.

When it spreads

Warm, wet conditions. Bacteria spread through splashing water, tools, and infected seed.

How to reduce it

Use disease-free seed and transplants, avoid handling wet plants, and rotate crops.

Fungal

Target spot

Corynespora cassiicola
What it looks like

Brown spots with light centers and ring patterns, similar to early blight. May also appear on stems and fruit.

When it spreads

Warm, humid weather with long periods of leaf wetness.

How to reduce it

Space and prune plants for airflow, remove infected leaves and debris, water at the base, and rotate crops.

Viral

Yellow leaf curl virus

Spread by whiteflies
What it looks like

Leaves curl upward and turn yellow at the edges, stay small, and the whole plant looks stunted.

When it spreads

Whiteflies carry the virus from plant to plant, mostly in warm weather.

How to reduce it

No cure. Control whiteflies, use resistant varieties and insect nets, and remove infected plants early.

Viral

Mosaic virus

Tomato mosaic virus
What it looks like

A mottled pattern of light and dark green patches, with leaves that may look wrinkled, narrow or twisted.

When it spreads

Spreads by touch through hands, tools and infected seed, and survives on surfaces for a long time.

How to reduce it

Use clean seed, wash hands and disinfect tools, remove infected plants, and choose resistant varieties.

Pest

Spider mites

Tiny pests, not a disease
What it looks like

Many tiny yellow or pale speckles on the leaf, which can turn bronze and dry out. Fine webbing may appear underneath.

When it spreads

Hot, dry weather, when mite numbers grow quickly.

How to reduce it

Rinse leaves with water, keep plants well watered, use insecticidal soap, and protect natural predators.

Healthy

Healthy leaf

No disease present
What it looks like

Even green color, a smooth surface, and no spots, mold, or curling.

Why it matters

the model needs healthy leaves as a class to tell a normal leaf from a diseased one.

How to keep it that way

Water at the base, give plants room to breathe, and check leaves regularly for early signs.
